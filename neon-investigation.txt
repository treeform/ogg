NEON investigation on Apple M1, Nim 2.2.10, release builds

AVX2 and current NEON do not have equal coverage. AVX2 accelerates Vorbis
FFT stages, dense Theora IDCT, and Theora reconstruction. NEON currently
accelerates only FFT stages. Video-only fixtures cannot benefit from this.

Disassembly of the oggNoSimd MDCT binary shows fmul.2d, fmla.2d, fadd.2d,
and fsub.2d in the FFT loop: the compiler already uses NEON. oggNoSimd
turns off explicit kernels; it does not disable compiler vectorization.

Controlled source experiment: replacing deinterleaving vld2/vst2 with
contiguous loads/stores plus unzip/zip improved isolated MDCT timing.
Explicit fused operations improved it further. These experiments identify
a costly instruction choice in the initial implementation, but do not
measure individual instruction latency or prove a hardware bottleneck.

Initial deinterleaving version in earlier nine-process measurements:
492.9, 2240.8, 10062.7 ns for the same three sizes.

Fused operations can change low-order floating-point bits. Existing dense
and sparse MDCT tests cover every supported block size against the cosine
definition, and corpus tests compare decoded samples to reference output.

The original 1.1% whole-corpus slowdown is small; it should not be treated
as a statistically established regression. Full-corpus measurements are
medians of eight alternating batches; fixtures are preloaded in memory.
See neon-benchmark-final.txt for final whole-decoder comparison.
